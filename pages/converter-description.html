The Converter turns a map from one format into another. It does not hold a map strictly to the standards. Common deviations are accepted where their meaning is clear, so that the conversion is as useful as possible, and whatever is left out is listed. It runs in the browser, and the files never leave the computer. The annotation (HGNC, UniProt, ChEBI) goes with the map. In GraphML it is read from the node properties, or from the node links when a map has none.

Each section below has the same parts. Input: the format, its version and the file extensions accepted. Check: what is checked before conversion, if anything. Output: one line per download button, as "button label, file name: format and version. Content. When given, if not always." Not converted: what is left out and where it is listed. <map> is the input file name without its extension.

From GraphML

Input: yEd GraphML (.graphml), an SBGN Process Description map.

Check: the SBGN Process Description rules, with these exceptions:

  • a phenotype may also stand for a perturbing agent or a submap
  • stimulation, inhibition, modulation and trigger may connect two entities directly (CellDesigner’s Reduced Notation)

Elements that break the rules are left out of the converted files.

Output:

  • CellDesigner, <map>-straight.xml: CellDesigner 4.0 (SBML Level 2 Version 4 with the CellDesigner extension). Each reaction a straight line between the centres of its main reactant and product, the process square midway.
  • CellDesigner (advanced), <map>-advanced.xml: CellDesigner 4.0. Process squares, bends and modifier arcs where the GraphML has them.
  • GraphML (corrected), <map>-corrected.graphml: yEd GraphML. The map redrawn with the SBGN palette, its shapes recognised by their outline. Given only when the map is drawn with other shapes.
  • SBGN-ML, <map>.sbgn: SBGN-ML 0.3, Process Description. Colours in the SBML render extension, annotation as RDF in each glyph. A phenotype with an arc leaving it is left out with its arcs: an SBGN phenotype takes no outgoing arc, and the converter does not decide whether it is a perturbing agent or a submap.
  • GraphML (marked), red button, <map>-marked.graphml: yEd GraphML. The input map with the elements that break the rules drawn red, the reason shown on hover in yEd. Given only when the check finds problems.

Not converted: the elements that break the rules, listed under the map in red, one per line with the element's id, label (an arc by its ends) and reason. Phenotypes left out of the SBGN-ML are listed under the map with their id and label. Equivalence operators are left out of the CellDesigner files, which have no such element, and kept in the SBGN-ML.

From CellDesigner

Input: CellDesigner 4.x (.xml), SBML Level 2 Version 4 with the CellDesigner extension.

Check: none.

Output:

  • GraphML, <map>-straight.graphml: yEd GraphML in our conventions. Arcs at node centres, metabolites at the yEd size.
  • GraphML (advanced), <map>-advanced.graphml: yEd GraphML with the CellDesigner geometry. CellDesigner's node sizes, arcs at its anchor points, modifier arcs ending beside the process square.

Not converted: text and shapes drawn on layers, groups, compartments drawn as an edge line, and state variables on a complex, listed under the map.

From SBGN-ML

Input: SBGN-ML 0.3 or SBGN-ML 0.2 (.sbgn), Process Description. Also a Newt file (.nwt): SBGN-ML with Newt's own settings added, converted the same way. An Activity Flow map is read too and drawn the CellDesigner way: each activity as the entity it comes from, a protein's activity as a protein, the influences as arcs between them.

Check: none.

Output:

  • GraphML, <map>.graphml: yEd GraphML.
  • CellDesigner, <map>-straight.xml: CellDesigner 4.0. Each reaction a straight line between the centres of its main reactant and product, the process square midway.
  • CellDesigner (advanced), <map>-advanced.xml: CellDesigner 4.0. Process squares, bends and modifier arcs where the SBGN-ML has them.

The CellDesigner files are converted from the SBGN-ML, not from the GraphML.

Not converted: glyph and arc classes the converter does not read, and clone markers, left out of all three files and listed under the map with their class and ids. A perturbing agent becomes a phenotype in all three files: our maps, like CellDesigner, have one glyph for both. An Activity Flow delay is kept in the GraphML as a logic operator labelled τ. The CellDesigner files have no delay, so there its input is joined to its target. An Activity Flow submap becomes a phenotype with its name, without its terminals and tags. Its equivalence arcs become plain lines to that phenotype in the GraphML. Equivalence operators are left out of the CellDesigner files, which have no such element, and kept in the GraphML.

Standard node sizes

Purpose: for maps drawn with a font that is too large or too small for their shapes, so the shapes do not look as they should, for example rounded corners that cannot be told from sharp ones, or arrowheads too small to see.

Applies to: the GraphML made from CellDesigner or SBGN-ML, when the box is ticked.

Sizes: proteins 80 x 40, metabolite circles 36 x 36, metabolite stadiums sized to the name. A metabolite keeps its shape. The map is rescaled if its objects are much larger or smaller.

Loading

As of: 17 October 2026, Pyodide 0.26.4.

Size: about 7 MB compressed (about 20 MB unpacked), downloaded on the first visit, then taken from the browser cache. Almost all of it is Python running in the browser (Pyodide): the Python engine 3.0 MB, its standard library 2.3 MB and the XML library lxml 1.4 MB. The converter itself is 0.05 MB.

Time: estimated 3 to 5 seconds on the first visit over a fast connection, about 10 seconds over a slow one (10 Mbit/s). Later visits need only the start of Python, 1 to 4 seconds. Loading starts as soon as the page opens. Once Python runs, a conversion takes a moment.
