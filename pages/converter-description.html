The converter turns a map from one format into another. It does not hold a map strictly to the standards. Common deviations are accepted where their meaning is clear, so that the conversion is as useful as possible, and whatever is left out is listed. It runs in the browser, and the files never leave the computer. The annotation (HGNC, UniProt, ChEBI) is converted with the map. In GraphML it is read from the node properties, or from the node links when a map has none.

Each section below has the same parts: Input (format, version, file extensions), Check (what is checked before conversion), Output (one row per download button) and Not converted (what is left out). Each file is named after the input file and its button, for example glycolysis-straight.xml for CellDesigner (straight) from glycolysis.graphml, and glycolysis.xml for CellDesigner.

Layouts: the files without a variant keep the layout of the input map as closely as possible: node positions, process ports and the key bends. Straight is the simpler fallback, for a map whose drawing does not convert well: every arc a straight line between node centres, for laying out by hand. In CellDesigner each process square sits midway between its main reactant and product. In both CellDesigner files a few nodes move a little so that lines meet straight, unless Move nodes slightly to straighten lines is unticked.

From GraphML

Input: yEd GraphML (.graphml), an SBGN Process Description map.

Check: the SBGN Process Description rules, with two exceptions: 1) a phenotype may also stand for a perturbing agent or a submap, and 2) stimulation, inhibition, modulation and trigger may connect two entities directly (CellDesigner’s Reduced Notation). Elements that break the rules are left out of the converted files.

CellDesigner 4.0 is SBML Level 2 Version 4 with the CellDesigner extension. A molecule drawn more than once is one CellDesigner species with one alias per drawing, as CellDesigner saves it. Two boxes are the same species when they have the same identifier (HGNC, UniProt or ChEBI, else the label), state, compartment and glyph. Both SBGN-ML files have their colours in the SBML render extension and the annotation as RDF in each glyph. A phenotype with an arc leaving it is left out of them with its arcs: an SBGN phenotype takes no outgoing arc, and the converter does not decide whether it is a perturbing agent or a submap.

In both SBGN-ML files every process and logic gate sits in a compartment, and each compartment is as large as Newt needs to keep it as drawn: Newt leaves a margin inside a compartment and otherwise redraws it around its contents. Edges aligned in the map stay aligned. Where two compartments' contents are too close for that margin, everything past their shared edge moves on by the missing distance, noted under the map.

Not converted: the elements that break the rules, listed under the map in red, one per line with the element's id, label (an arc by its ends) and reason. Phenotypes left out of the SBGN-ML are listed under the map with their id and label. Equivalence operators are left out of the CellDesigner files, which have no such element, and kept in the SBGN-ML.

From CellDesigner

Input: CellDesigner 4.x (.xml), SBML Level 2 Version 4 with the CellDesigner extension. Files of older CellDesigner versions, without the extension element, are not read.

Check: none.

Output:

GraphMLstraightyEd GraphML (.graphml)Our conventions: arcs at node centres, metabolites at the yEd size.
GraphMLyEd GraphML (.graphml)The CellDesigner geometry: its node sizes, arcs at its anchor points, modifier arcs ending beside the process square.
SBGN-MLstraightSBGN-ML 0.3 (.sbgn)Process DescriptionEach arc a straight line from centre to centre, each process midway between its main reactant and product, for improving by hand.
SBGN-MLSBGN-ML 0.3 (.sbgn)Process DescriptionThe CellDesigner drawing kept: its node sizes, process positions and the bends that change the course of a line by more than 20 px or keep it off a node.

Both SBGN-ML files are made from the CellDesigner file directly, with their colours in the SBML render extension and the annotation as RDF in each glyph. A phenotype with an arc leaving it is left out with its arcs, as from GraphML.

Both SBGN-ML files give a homodimer its N:x unit, N:2 included. Since CellDesigner files carry no site names, their state variables have none.

Not converted: text and shapes drawn on layers, groups, compartments drawn as an edge line, and state variables on a complex, listed under the map.

From SBGN-ML

Input: SBGN-ML 0.3 or SBGN-ML 0.2 (.sbgn), Process Description. Also a Newt file (.nwt): SBGN-ML with Newt's own settings added, converted the same way. An Activity Flow map is read too and drawn the CellDesigner way: each activity as the entity it comes from, a protein's activity as a protein, the influences as arcs between them. In a map that mixes Process Description and Activity Flow, an activity joined to its entity by a performance arc is drawn as that entity, one node for the molecule and its activity, as in CellDesigner's reduced notation.

Check: none.

Output:

ButtonFileContent
GraphMLyEd GraphML (.graphml)
CellDesignerstraightCellDesigner 4.x (.xml)Each reaction a straight line between the centres of its main reactant and product, the process square midway.
CellDesignerCellDesigner 4.x (.xml)Process squares, bends and modifier arcs where the SBGN-ML has them.

The CellDesigner files are converted from the SBGN-ML, not from the GraphML.

Not converted: glyph and arc classes the converter does not read, and clone markers, left out of all three files and listed under the map with their class and ids. A perturbing agent becomes a phenotype in all three files: our maps, like CellDesigner, have one glyph for both. An Activity Flow delay is kept in the GraphML as a logic operator labelled τ. The CellDesigner files have no delay, so there its input is joined to its target. An Activity Flow submap becomes a phenotype with its name, without its terminals and tags. Its equivalence arcs become plain lines to that phenotype in the GraphML. Equivalence operators are left out of the CellDesigner files, which have no such element, and kept in the GraphML.

Standard node sizes

Purpose: for maps drawn with a font that is too large or too small for their shapes, so the shapes do not look as they should, for example rounded corners that cannot be told from sharp ones, or arrowheads too small to see.

Applies to: the GraphML made from CellDesigner or SBGN-ML, when the box is ticked.

Sizes: proteins 80 x 40, metabolite circles 36 x 36, metabolite stadiums sized to the name. A metabolite keeps its shape. The map is rescaled if its objects are much larger or smaller.

Move nodes slightly to straighten lines

Purpose: lines that meet straight in CellDesigner, which draws a line only to a node's centre or its fixed anchor points. Ticked by default. Untick it for a map whose node positions must stay exactly as drawn.

Applies to: both CellDesigner files, when the box is ticked.

Moves: three small corrections make lines meet straight. A main line up to 10 px off the horizontal or vertical is straightened. An end that meets a larger node beside its centre moves onto that node's centre line. A modifier up to 15 px off its process square moves onto it. Each move is listed under the map with the node and the distance. Metabolite circles, the size of a multimer and the room for a complex's name below its members are CellDesigner's own and are kept either way.

Loading

Updated: 19 October 2026, Pyodide 0.26.4.

Size: about 7 MB compressed (about 20 MB unpacked), downloaded on the first visit, then taken from the browser cache. Almost all of it is Python running in the browser (Pyodide): the Python engine 3.0 MB, its standard library 2.3 MB and the XML library lxml 1.4 MB. The converter itself is 0.06 MB.

Time: estimated 3 to 5 seconds on the first visit over a fast connection, about 10 seconds over a slow one (10 Mbit/s). Later visits need only the start of Python, 1 to 4 seconds. Loading starts as soon as the page opens. Once Python runs, a conversion takes a moment.
