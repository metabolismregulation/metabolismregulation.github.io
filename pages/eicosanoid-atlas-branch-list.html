The branching organisation as the viewer's list: two levels shown, a numbered group and its items, and the deeper layers carried by prefixes, as folders named with prefixes keep a flat tree readable. The group's number gives the domain (0x source, 1x mediators, 9x control and assemblies). The item's prefix gives its branch point, and so the class and the enzyme above it. The fatty acid series sits inside the item, in tabs or rows. The full tree is on Branch layers.

The list

Numbers: estimated processes, each item drawn from its branch point to its receptors. File names: the group's number, a family code and the item (10-pg-pge2), a tab adds the fatty acid (10-pg-pge2-aa). A fatty acid gets a tab from about 8 processes, fewer and it is a row in the same map.

Fatty acids: a tab or a row, not a level

The same enzymes take every polyunsaturated fatty acid whose double bonds sit where the enzyme needs them. Which branches a fatty acid has is set by its double bonds. How much it makes is set by how much of it the membrane holds and how well each enzyme takes it. The positions in the headers hold for the C20 fatty acids. DHA, two carbons longer, is oxidised at the matching positions counted from its methyl end.

So EPA is not a smaller copy of AA but the same chemistry at lower amounts: less EPA in the membrane on most diets, weaker turnover by COX-1 and LTA4 hydrolase, and its products less studied, with fewer standards and assays. The P450 epoxygenases are the exception, taking EPA and DHA at least as well as AA. EPA's extra double bond (Δ17) also opens branches AA lacks: 17,18-EEQ and 18-HEPE, the start of the E-series resolvins. MetaCore's Prostaglandin 1, 2 and 3 are this table read by rows, one fatty acid at a time.
