A proposal for how the atlas is organised: the list on the atlas page and the regions of a future master map follow the same blocks. It starts from fatty acid synthesis and goes past the eicosanoids in the strict sense (C20) to the mediators made from the same fatty acids, by the same enzymes or in the same reaction.

Fatty acid by branch

Every piece is one branch for one fatty acid. Numbers: reactions found in the sources (KEGG, Reactome, Rhea, Recon3D, MetaCore, the AsthmaMap and the OA map) for each pair. Dark: a piece drawn on the atlas page now (its number is still the count in the sources). Striped: known from the literature, not yet searched in the databases. Empty: little or no chemistry known, or not searched.

The same grid is the floor plan of the master map: fatty acids as rows (n-6 above n-3, the elongation chain running down the supply column on the left), branches as columns, so that each enzyme sits in one column for all its substrates and EPA's products sit under AA's. The pro-resolving mediators sit between the lipoxygenase columns, where two of them meet. Transport and the receptors run along the right edge, the plasma membrane, and regulation along the bottom.

The list: groups and items

Two levels: a group, then its items. A fatty acid is a tab of an item, as now (Prostanoids: AA, EPA, DGLA). A large item is cut into parts named by a third word (prostanoids-aa-pge2), still one item in the list. File names put the branch first and the fatty acid last (prostanoids-aa, leukotrienes-epa), so the pieces of one branch sort together.

Fatty acid supply new

Where the fatty acids come from. The starting point of the whole map.

  • De novo fatty acid synthesis fa-synthesis
    citrate to acetyl-CoA (ACLY), malonyl-CoA (ACC), palmitate (FASN)
  • Elongation and desaturation of saturated and monounsaturated fatty acids fa-elongation
    ELOVL6 (palmitate to stearate), SCD1 (stearate to oleate), ELOVL1, 3, 7 (very long chains)
  • n-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids pufa-n6
    LA, GLA, DGLA, AA (FADS2, ELOVL5, FADS1), then adrenic acid and DPA n-6 (ELOVL2, ELOVL5)
  • n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids pufa-n3
    ALA, SDA, ETA, EPA, DPA, then 24:5, 24:6 (ELOVL2, FADS2) and DHA by peroxisomal β-oxidation (ACOX1, HSD17B4)
  • Mead acid pufa-n9
    oleate to 20:3 n-9 by the same desaturases and elongase when LA and ALA are short
  • Uptake and carriers fa-uptake
    CD36, FATPs (SLC27A), FABPs, MFSD2A (DHA as lysophosphatidylcholine into the brain)

Membrane pool and release new

Where the PUFAs wait, and how they come out. Release was one reaction so far.

  • Acyl-CoA synthetases activation
    ACSL4 (prefers AA and EPA), ACSL1, ACSL3
  • Phospholipid remodelling (Lands cycle) remodelling
    LPCAT3 (PC, PE), MBOAT7 (AA into PI), CoA-independent transacylation, plasmalogens
  • Phospholipases A2 release-pla2
    tabs: AA, EPA, DHA
    cPLA2α (PLA2G4A), secreted PLA2s (PLA2G2A, PLA2G5, PLA2G10), iPLA2β (PLA2G6)
  • Release from lipid droplets release-lipid-droplets
    ATGL and HSL on triacylglycerols, a source of AA in mast cells and neutrophils
  • Release through 2-AG release-2ag
    PLCβ, DAG lipase, MAGL: the main AA source for prostaglandins in brain, liver and lung
  • PAF paf
    cPLA2α on alkyl-arachidonoyl-PC gives AA and lyso-PAF, LPCAT2 makes PAF, PAF acetylhydrolase (PLA2G7) ends it

Bridges worth drawing

  • Endocannabinoids feed eicosanoids. MAGL turns 2-AG into arachidonic acid, and in brain, liver and lung this, not cPLA2α, supplies most of the AA for prostaglandins. COX-2 also oxygenates 2-AG and anandamide directly.
  • PAF is the twin product of cPLA2α. The same hydrolysis of alkyl-arachidonoyl-PC gives arachidonic acid and lyso-PAF, so PAF and the eicosanoids come from one reaction in one cell.
  • Ferroptosis uses the eicosanoid enzymes. ACSL4 and LPCAT3 put AA and adrenic acid into PE, ALOX15 oxidises them in the membrane, GPX4 stops it. The same enzymes as the release and 15-LOX pieces, a different end.
  • Mead acid shows the supply chain at work. When LA and ALA are short, the same desaturases and elongase make 20:3 n-9 from oleate, and 5-LOX turns it into the 3-series leukotrienes.
  • Fish oil works by competition. EPA and DHA displace AA in the membrane and at COX and 5-LOX, giving 3- and 5-series products and the pro-resolving mediators: a cross-row comparison the grid shows directly.
  • The skin barrier is a lipoxygenase pathway. ALOX12B and ALOXE3 oxidise the linoleate of acylceramides, and losing either gives congenital ichthyosis.
  • Peroxide tone links to redox. COX and LOX start only with a hydroperoxide present, so peroxiredoxins and glutathione peroxidases set the threshold (the MRegAtlas peroxiredoxin map).
  • Nitric oxide meets fatty acids. Nitrogen dioxide from nitric oxide nitrates oleate and linoleate into electrophilic nitro fatty acids that act on PPARγ and KEAP1 (the MRegAtlas iNOS map).
