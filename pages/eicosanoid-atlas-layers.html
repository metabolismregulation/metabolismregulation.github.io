An alternative to the two-level list: every step placed in a tree of eight layers, from the domain down to the single process. The other pages show the same atlas by enzyme branch, by branching and as the viewer list.

Numbers are estimated processes. The last layer, single steps, is written out for two examples: PGE2 from AA and LTB4 from AA (Oxygenation, then Cyclooxygenase or Lipoxygenase). Elsewhere the tree stops at the stage, with the number of its processes. A branch stops early where a layer has nothing to sort (Source and Control have fewer layers).

The tree

Click a block to zoom in, the path above to zoom out. Width of a column: one layer. Height of a block: its processes.

What many layers give, and cost

  • Any layer can be the map. A family, a mediator or a single stage: the viewer shows the processes under the block picked, so a reader chooses the size of the map. The list need not decide it once for all.
  • The vertical slice is kept. Each mediator holds its synthesis, transport, inactivation and receptors, and the enzyme and route above it keep the enzyme view. Both earlier proposals sit in one tree: by enzyme reads it at layers 2 to 3, by family at layer 4.
  • Fatty acids sit low, under the mediator, so PGE1, PGE2 and PGE3 stay together. Comparing all AA with all EPA needs a view across the tree, as the grid of the structure page does.
  • A tree places each step once. Lipoxins need two lipoxygenases and sit under a node of their own. 5-HETE sits under 5-LOX, not with 12- and 15-HETE. Two homes need a link, not a copy.
  • Deep paths are long to click through. Eight layers suit navigation by zooming or search. As a plain list they are too deep: a viewer would show layers 1 to 4 as headings and groups and the rest inside the map.
