The organisation of the branching page as a tree of layers: families, as the pathway resources group the eicosanoids, then their classes, the branch points, the mediators and their life. The layers page shows the tree of the enzyme view.

Numbers are estimated processes. Steps, the last layer, are written out for one example: PGE2 from AA with its receptors, the EP2 receptor down to its steps (Mediators, Prostaglandins and thromboxanes, Prostaglandins, PGH2, PGE2). Elsewhere the tree stops one layer higher, with the number of processes. Source and Control have fewer layers. Receptors sit under the mediator, not under a fatty acid: one receptor serves the whole series (EP2 for PGE1 to PGE3). A receptor with several ligands (BLT1, TP, CB1, PPARγ) counts its signalling once and only the binding under a further ligand.

The tree

Click a block to zoom in, the path above to zoom out. Width of a column: one layer. Height of a block: its processes.

Compared with the enzyme tree

  • Families come first, so a reader looking for prostaglandins or resolvins finds them in one place, as in Reactome and in reviews. The enzyme appears only in the branch point (PGH2 means cyclooxygenase, 17-HpDHA means 15-lipoxygenase).
  • Hydroxy acids stay one family, 5-, 12-, 15- and ω-hydroxy acids side by side, where the enzyme tree splits them over three lipoxygenases and the P450 enzymes.
  • Lipoxins sit with the pro-resolving mediators, not under a node of two lipoxygenases.
  • Branch points repeat. PGH2 appears under prostaglandins, prostacyclin and thromboxanes, 17-HpDHA under D-series resolvins and protectins. In the enzyme tree each enzyme appears once. Here it is a label, the same metabolite drawn in each map.
  • One layer more for the list. Family and class make two group levels above the mediator. Where a class has a single branch point, the two collapse in the list (Prostacyclin: PGH2: PGI2).
