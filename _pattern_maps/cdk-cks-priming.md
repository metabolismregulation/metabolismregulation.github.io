A first phosphorylation can prepare a protein for more. The cyclin-Cdk-Cks complex phosphorylates a threonine in a disordered region of its substrate, its Cks subunit binds the phosphothreonine, and the docked kinase then phosphorylates nearby serines and threonines. The priming site holds the kinase close to the other sites, a simple alternative to allostery that makes multisite phosphorylation cooperative. Glycogen synthase kinase 3 and casein kinase 1 also depend on priming, through a pocket in the kinase domain itself.

**Added for the drawing.** The identifiers of the human cell cycle Cdks (CDK1, CDK2) and of Cks1 and 2 (CKS1B, CKS2), the vertebrate forms the book names. Cyclin and the substrate are generic and have no identifiers.

**Not in the book, so not drawn.** The release of the substrate after the second phosphorylation.
