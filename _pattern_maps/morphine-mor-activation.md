An agonist first binds its receptor and the bound receptor then changes to its active conformation, a second equilibrium. How far that second step goes sets the drug's efficacy. Morphine, heroin and fentanyl are full agonists, with maximal binding giving maximal activation. Buprenorphine is a high-affinity partial agonist, with maximal binding giving less than maximal activation, which is why it is used to treat opioid addiction. An antagonist such as naloxone binds without promoting the change at all.
