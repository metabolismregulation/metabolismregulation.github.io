MEK1 is phosphorylated by Raf or Mos on two sites, in order, and is already fully active after the first. The second site is inessential, but it shapes the response: MEK1 is easy to turn on and hard to turn off, since both sites must be removed. ERK2, which needs both its sites, is the reverse, hard to turn on and easy to turn off. Extra inessential sites add a time lag to inactivation and make the response more switch-like.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, sections 5.12 and 5.13, inessential sites, with MEK1 as the example (the book's second example, the pentameric nicotinic acetylcholine receptor, is not drawn).

**From the book in this map.** Raf phosphorylating MEK1 on its two sites, and both phosphorylated forms of MEK1 activating ERK2. Drawn new for this page.

**Added for the drawing.** ERK2's two phosphorylations as one step (the two steps apart are [Multisite phosphorylation](/pattern-atlas/#erk2-multisite-phosphorylation)). The identifiers are ARAF, BRAF and RAF1 for Raf, MAP2K1 and MAPK1.
