The branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase complex (BCKDH) is inactivated by the kinase BCKDK and reactivated by the phosphatase PPM1K. The keto acids made from leucine, isoleucine and valine inhibit BCKDK, and α-ketoisocaproate also stimulates the reactivation by PPM1K. One input turns the kinase down and the phosphatase up, both towards the active enzyme, so an excess of branched-chain amino acids speeds up their own breakdown. Regulating the two opposing enzymes in opposite directions can make the response steeper than acting on one of them (Ferrell 2022, section 5.15).
