Liquid droplets without a membrane, such as P granules, nucleoli and centrosomes, form by phase separation. A protein exchanges slowly between the bulk cytoplasm and a droplet of which it is the main component, and the more protein the droplet holds, the faster it takes up more. This positive feedback does not make a bistable switch. It gives a threshold, a transcritical bifurcation: below a critical concentration no droplet grows, above it a droplet grows along a sigmoidal time course. Vesicle formation and the spread of an epidemic follow the same logic.
