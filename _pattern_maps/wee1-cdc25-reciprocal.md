The book's example (Ferrell 2022, section 5.15). Wee1 phosphorylates the master mitotic regulator cyclin B-Cdk1 and blocks mitotic entry, and the phosphatase Cdc25 removes that phosphate. Stresses such as DNA damage lead to the phosphorylation and activation of Wee1 and to the phosphorylation and inhibition of Cdc25. One input turns up the enzyme that inactivates Cdk1 and turns down the one that reactivates it, so both act in the same direction. Regulating the two opposing enzymes in this way can make the response of Cdk1 more switch-like than acting on one of them.

The book does not name the kinases between DNA damage and the two enzymes, so DNA damage acts on their phosphorylation directly.

Also in the MRegAtlas: AMP stimulates the phosphorylation of AMPK and inhibits its dephosphorylation ([Mevalonate biosynthesis](/atlas/#mevalonate)), and the branched-chain keto acids inhibit the kinase BCKDK and stimulate the phosphatase PPM1K ([BCAA catabolism](/atlas/#bcaa)).
