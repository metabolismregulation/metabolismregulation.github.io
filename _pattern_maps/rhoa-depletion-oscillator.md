Waves of cortical contraction in oocytes and eggs arise from pulses of RhoA activity. RhoA cycles through three states. Inactive GDP-bound RhoA in the cytoplasm binds the membrane, a guanine nucleotide exchange factor converts it to active GTP-bound RhoA with positive feedback from active RhoA, and GTP hydrolysis and release from the membrane return it to the cytoplasm. No step inhibits RhoA. The active form spikes and then runs out because the membrane-bound inactive form is used up, and depletion takes the place of negative feedback in a relaxation oscillator.
