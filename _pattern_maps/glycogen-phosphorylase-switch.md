Phosphorylase kinase phosphorylates muscle glycogen phosphorylase (PYGM), and protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) removes the phosphate. The phosphorylated enzyme breaks glycogen down to glucose 1-phosphate. Phosphorylase kinase itself is turned on by Ca2+ binding to its calmodulin subunit and by PKA phosphorylation.

When the kinase and the phosphatase both work close to saturation, the share of phosphorylated PYGM changes steeply over a narrow range of kinase activity, a switch built from enzyme saturation without cooperativity.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, sections 5.6 and 5.7, zero-order ultrasensitivity. The book's example is the phosphorylation of muscle glycogen phosphorylase by phosphorylase kinase against phosphorylase phosphatase, in reconstituted enzymes: the response was sigmoidal at 70 µM phosphorylase, a few times above the enzymes' KM values, but not at 20 µM, and phosphorylase may reach 100 µM in skeletal muscle.
