EGFR ligands are provided locally: they are cut from precursor proteins on the surface of one cell and spread to receptors on nearby cells. When the receptor binds with high affinity, it takes up much of the ligand released, and the free ligand falls below the amount added. Ligand depletion turns a Michaelian response into a near-linear one. With negative cooperativity in the dimeric receptor, as in the EGF receptor, depletion can instead create a threshold.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, sections 4.2 to 4.4, ligand depletion, its effect on cooperative receptors, and the locally provided EGFR ligands.

**From the book in this map.** The release of EGF from its precursor and its binding to the receptor dimer, one molecule at a time. Drawn new for this page.

**Not in the book, so not drawn.** The protease that releases EGF (the metalloproteases of [Excitability](/pattern-atlas/#erk-excitability)), so the release has no catalyst, and TGFα and the other ligands. The identifiers are EGF and EGFR.

**MRegAtlas.** The book's example overlaps with [Phosphoinositides](/atlas/#phosphoinositides) and [Pyrimidine biosynthesis](/atlas/#pyrimidine), which draw EGF bound to EGFR.
