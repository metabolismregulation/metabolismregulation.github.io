Negative feedback makes a steady state more stable and lets the system reach it faster. In the book's model x makes y, y is destroyed, and y binds x and inhibits it. A rise in y slows its own production and a fall speeds it, so the system returns quickly after a disturbance. For the same steady state and flux, the stronger the feedback, the faster the approach.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, sections 11.1 and 11.2, negative feedback, stability and speed, in a general production and destruction model.

**Not in the book, so not drawn.** Particular molecules: x and y are drawn as unspecified entities, with no identifiers.

**MRegAtlas.** A product holding back its own production is in [SREBP signalling](/atlas/#srebp), where sterol with INSIG holds SCAP and SREBP2, the factor that drives sterol synthesis. A negative feedback through an enzyme is in [cAMP hydrolysis](/atlas/#camp).
