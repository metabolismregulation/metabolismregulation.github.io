Active Raf phosphorylates and activates MEK, and active MEK phosphorylates and activates ERK, which goes on to its targets, such as transcription factors. Each kinase acts on many molecules of the next, so a cascade reaches an abundant target faster than one kinase could: by the book's estimate, three tiers do in under 30 seconds what a single kinase would need about 17 minutes for. Michaelian steps passed down a cascade make the signal more graded, and ultrasensitive steps, such as the two phosphorylations of MEK and of ERK, can restore or increase its decisiveness.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, sections 7.1 to 7.4, cascades and amplification, with the MAP kinase cascade Raf, MEK and ERK as the example (in Xenopus oocyte extracts, Mos, MEK1 and ERK2, section 7.5).

**From the book in this map.** Raf, MEK and ERK, MEK and ERK each activated by two phosphorylations. Drawn new for this page, the two phosphorylations as one step each (the multisite map shows them apart).

**Not in the book, so not drawn.** The phosphatases and the terminal effectors, which the book does not name.

**MRegAtlas.** The book's example overlaps with [Eicosanoids](/atlas/#eicosanoids) (PKC, RAF1, MEK1/2, ERK1/2 and cPLA2) and [Pyrimidine biosynthesis](/atlas/#pyrimidine) (EGF, RAS, RAF1, MEK1/2, ERK1/2 and CAD). Another cascade is in [Glycogen metabolism (muscle)](/atlas/#glycogen-muscle), from epinephrine through cAMP, PKA and phosphorylase kinase to glycogen phosphorylase.
