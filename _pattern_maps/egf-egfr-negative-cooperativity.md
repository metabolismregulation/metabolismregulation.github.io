The EGF receptor works as a dimer that binds two EGF molecules, one at a time. With negative cooperativity the first binding makes the second less favourable, and the binding curve is more graded than a Michaelian one, a subsensitive response. The interaction of the Drosophila EGF homologue Spitz with the Drosophila receptor shows it clearly: the second molecule binds with an affinity some 370 times lower than the first.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, sections 3.5 and 3.6, negative cooperativity in EGF binding to the dimeric receptor, measured for Drosophila Spitz and EGFR.

**From the book in this map.** The receptor dimer binding one EGF and then a second, both steps reversible. Drawn new for this page.

**Added for the drawing.** The human EGF and EGFR, the book's general example, where the binding constants were measured with the Drosophila proteins. The counts as N:2, as on the MRegAtlas. The identifiers are EGF and EGFR.

**MRegAtlas.** The book's example overlaps with [Phosphoinositides](/atlas/#phosphoinositides), which draws the EGF:EGFR dimer, and [Pyrimidine biosynthesis](/atlas/#pyrimidine), which draws EGF-bound EGFR.
