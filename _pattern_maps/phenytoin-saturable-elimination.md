A drug taken in daily doses and eliminated by excretion and metabolism is a production and destruction system, like a second messenger. Most drugs are eliminated with first-order kinetics, far from saturating the kidney and liver, so their steady-state level rises in proportion to the dose. Phenytoin's elimination is close to saturation at therapeutic levels: a typical daily dose is 300 mg and the maximal elimination rate about 400 mg a day. A 50% higher dose, safe for carbamazepine, can drive phenytoin to toxic levels.
