Haemoglobin, a tetramer of two α and two β globins, binds four oxygen molecules, and its binding curve is sigmoidal: oxygen loads in the lungs and unloads in the tissues over a narrow range of pressures. In the model of Monod, Wyman and Changeux the tetramer switches between a tense state that binds oxygen with low affinity and a relaxed state that binds it with high affinity. Each oxygen bound pulls more tetramers to the relaxed state and so raises the affinity for the next one.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, sections 3.1 to 3.5, positive cooperativity, the Hill and MWC models, with oxygen binding to haemoglobin.

**From the book in this map.** Haemoglobin in the tense (T) and relaxed (R) states, free and bound to oxygen, and the four steps between them, all reversible. Drawn new for this page.

**Added for the drawing.** One oxygen per complex: the four oxygens bound one at a time are in the text, not drawn. Each binding has its own O2, so that both partners enter the step from one side. Haemoglobin stays one box, as the book names it, without identifiers. Dioxygen in ChEBI.

**Not in the book, so not drawn.** The KNF model, the book's other account of the same curve.
