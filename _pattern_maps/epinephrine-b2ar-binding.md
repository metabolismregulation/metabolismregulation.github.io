The hormone epinephrine binds the β2-adrenergic receptor, one molecule to one receptor, and the complex forms and falls apart until the two rates balance. At equilibrium the fraction of receptors bound follows a hyperbolic, Michaelian curve of the hormone concentration, half full at the dissociation constant. Most of the response is graded: an 81-fold rise in hormone takes the receptor from 10% to 90% occupied.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, sections 2.1 to 2.8, the β2-adrenergic receptor and its agonist epinephrine as the example of simple binding.

**From the book in this map.** Epinephrine, the receptor and their complex, the binding drawn as reversible. Drawn new for this page.

**Not in the book, so not drawn.** The receptor's activation and the G protein, the next steps (the map of binding and activation as separate steps, and [State-dependent inactivation](/pattern-atlas/#b2ar-arrestin-inactivation)). The identifiers are ADRB2 and epinephrine in ChEBI.
