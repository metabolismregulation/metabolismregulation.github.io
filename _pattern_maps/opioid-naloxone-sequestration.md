Heroin, an opioid agonist, binds the µ-opioid receptor. Naloxone, a high-affinity antagonist, binds the same receptor into a complex that goes no further, so the two compete for it. A high-affinity inhibitor of this kind soaks up the first increments of agonist and builds a threshold into the response, and a sufficient dose of naloxone can block or reverse heroin intoxication.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, section 4.5, stoichiometric regulators competing with stoichiometric inhibitors.
