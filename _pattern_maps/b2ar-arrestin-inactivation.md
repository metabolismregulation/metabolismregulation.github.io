Epinephrine binds the β2-adrenergic receptor, and the active receptor makes Gsα exchange GDP for GTP. βARK phosphorylates the active receptor at its C-terminus and cytoplasmic loops, and the phosphorylated receptor binds β-arrestin, which stops it from activating G proteins. The receptor bound to β-arrestin activates another pathway, the ERK MAP kinase cascade. The result is a pulse of G-protein activation followed, after a lag, by ERK signalling.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, section 13.7, state-dependent inactivation, with the β-adrenergic receptor, βARK and β-arrestin as the example (the receptor and epinephrine in section 2.1). The book adds that the µ-opioid receptor works the same way, G-protein signalling giving analgesia and β-arrestin respiratory depression in mice, and that agonists biased to one pathway are known.

**From the book in this map.** Epinephrine, the receptor in its three states (bound, phosphorylated, bound to β-arrestin), βARK, β-arrestin, Gsα and ERK. Drawn new for this page.

**Added for the drawing.** Gsα with GDP and with GTP, standing for the G-protein activation the book describes, without the free GTP and GDP. ERK activation as a stimulation by the receptor bound to β-arrestin, since the book does not name the cascade between.

**MRegAtlas.** The book's example overlaps with [Glycogen metabolism (muscle)](/atlas/#glycogen-muscle), which draws epinephrine binding ADRB2 and the activation of Gsα. βARK and β-arrestin are not on the MRegAtlas.
