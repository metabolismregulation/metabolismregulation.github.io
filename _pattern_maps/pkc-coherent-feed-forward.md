The book's example (Ferrell 2022, section 5.14). A phospholipase C converts the inner leaflet phospholipid PI(4,5)P2 into diacylglycerol (DAG) and IP3. DAG binds classical protein kinase C (PKC) and helps recruit it to the plasma membrane. IP3 binds its receptor and releases Ca2+ from the endoplasmic reticulum, and Ca2+ then binds DAG-bound PKC and activates it. PLC reaches active PKC by two routes of the same sign, one through DAG and one through IP3 and Ca2+, so the active kinase needs both. Coherent feed-forward regulation of this kind can make the response ultrasensitive.

Also in the MRegAtlas: the eicosanoid maps start in the same way, PLC making DAG and IP3 and the IP3 receptor releasing Ca2+ ([Eicosanoids](/atlas/#eicosanoids)), and PKA acts on lipolysis by two routes, through hormone-sensitive lipase and through its complex with perilipin 1 ([Triacylglycerol hydrolysis](/atlas/#tag)).
