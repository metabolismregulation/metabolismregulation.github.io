Wee1 phosphorylates cyclin B-Cdk1 and keeps it inactive, and Cdc25 removes that phosphate. Active cyclin B-Cdk1 phosphorylates Cdc25 and activates it, a positive feedback loop, and phosphorylates Wee1 and inhibits it, a double-negative feedback loop. Both loops push the trigger of mitotic entry the same way. With both, the trigger turns off more completely and switches more decisively than with one, and it is more robust.

## Source

**Book.** Ferrell 2022, section 15.7, interlinked positive and double-negative feedback loops in the mitotic trigger of the embryonic cell cycle (the oscillator around it in sections 15.2 to 15.6). The book notes that in this case the two loops work at essentially the same speed.

**From the book in this map.** Cyclin B-Cdk1 active and phosphorylated, Wee1 and Cdc25 each in two states, and the four phosphorylations and dephosphorylations. Drawn new for this page.

**Not in the book, so not drawn.** The sites. The phosphorylations by Cdk1 drawn here are other sites than those of the DNA damage response in the reciprocal regulation map, and they act the other way. The cyclin synthesis and degradation of the full oscillator are left out.
