The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDH) converts pyruvate into acetyl-CoA. Pyruvate dehydrogenase kinases (PDK) inactivate it by phosphorylation, and the phosphatases PDP reactivate it. The share of active PDH is set by the balance of the two opposing enzymes, and each of them is a point of control (Ferrell 2022, section 5.1).

The products acetyl-CoA and NADH inhibit the complex directly and also stimulate PDK, so they act on the reaction and on the cycle in the same direction. Pyruvate inhibits PDK, Ca2+ stimulates PDP, and the drug dichloroacetate inhibits PDK.
