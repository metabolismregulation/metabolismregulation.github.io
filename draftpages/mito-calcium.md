## Selection

Selected by literature curation, as part of the extension of the Atlas. The transport steps and the two dehydrogenase reactions are in the Reactome-Recon material, but the calcium control drawn here is not.

## Description

Mitochondria turn a rise of calcium in the cytoplasm, caused by hormones or by contraction, into a faster supply of NADH and of ATP. Calcium crosses the inner membrane through the uniporter, a channel built around MCU with the small subunit EMRE, which the current needs, and the calcium-sensing subunits MICU1 and MICU2, which keep it shut until the cytosolic concentration rises (Kirichok 2004, Baughman 2011, De Stefani 2011, Sancak 2013, Kamer 2014). It leaves again through the sodium-calcium exchanger NCLX (Palty 2010). Inside, micromolar calcium activates three dehydrogenases of the matrix (McCormack 1990, Denton 2009). It lowers the Km of the 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex for its substrate without changing the maximal rate, acting at a site in the E1 subunit that some splice variants of brain and pancreatic islets lack (McCormack 1979, Armstrong 2014, Denton 2016). It lowers the Km of NAD-isocitrate dehydrogenase for isocitrate, but only when ADP is there as well (Denton 1978). And it makes the phosphatase PDP1 bind the core of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex about ten times better, which takes the phosphates off the E1 subunit and switches that complex on (Denton 1972, Chen 1996). The same two nucleotide signals run through all of it: ADP speeds the dehydrogenases up and NADH slows them down, so the matrix reads the demand for ATP and the supply of reducing power at once.
