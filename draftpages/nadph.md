## Description

NADPH is the reducing power behind fatty acid and cholesterol synthesis and behind the defence against oxidants. Most of it comes from the first step of the pentose phosphate pathway, in which glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase oxidises glucose 6-phosphate to 6-phosphoglucono-1,5-lactone and reduces NADP+ to NADPH. The enzyme works only when its subunits come together, and the monomer is inactive (Au 2000). Several marks decide this. O-GlcNAc on Ser84, placed by O-GlcNAc transferase, favours the active form and raises the flux in cells under low oxygen (Rao 2015). Acetylation of Lys403 keeps the subunits apart, and the deacetylase SIRT2 removes it and restores activity (Wang 2014, Xu 2016). In mitosis, polo-like kinase 1 phosphorylates Thr466 and also favours the active form (Ma 2017). The tumour suppressor p53 works the other way, binding the enzyme and holding it in a form that cannot work, which is separate from its role as a transcription factor (Jiang 2011). The supply of the cosubstrate is regulated as well. NAD kinase makes NADP+ from NAD+, and AKT phosphorylates it on three neighbouring serines, which raises its activity and enlarges the cellular NADP(H) pool (Hoxhaj 2019).
