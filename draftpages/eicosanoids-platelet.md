## Description

In platelets, thrombin releases arachidonate for thromboxane and prostaglandin synthesis. Thrombin cleaves and activates its two platelet receptors, PAR1 and PAR4, which activate Gαq. Gαq stimulates phospholipase Cβ2 and Cβ3, which split PI(4,5)P2 into IP3 and diacylglycerol. IP3, together with cytosolic Ca2+, opens the IP3 receptor and releases Ca2+ from the dense tubular system, the endoplasmic reticulum of the platelet. Diacylglycerol activates protein kinase C, which activates RAF1 and the ERK cascade. Ca2+ binds the C2 domain of cytosolic phospholipase A2 (cPLA2α, PLA2G4A), two ions per domain, and brings the enzyme to its membrane substrate, and PI(4,5)P2 raises its activity. In thrombin-stimulated platelets p38α phosphorylates cPLA2α at Ser505. At rest the annexin A2:S100A10 complex holds cPLA2α off the membrane, and MNK1 or a related kinase phosphorylates Ser727 and frees the enzyme. cPLA2α releases arachidonate from phosphatidylcholine, and cyclooxygenase-1, the only cyclooxygenase of mature platelets, turns it into prostaglandin G2 and H2. Pyrrophenone is a selective cPLA2α inhibitor.
