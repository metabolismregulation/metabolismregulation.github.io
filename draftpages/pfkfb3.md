## Selection

Selected by literature curation, as part of the extension of the Atlas. PFKFB3 is in neither version of the Reactome-Recon lists, which hold the liver isoenzyme PFKFB1 only, and none of the regulation drawn here is in Reactome.

## Description

Fructose 2,6-bisphosphate is the strongest allosteric activator of 6-phosphofructo-1-kinase and so sets the rate of glycolysis at its committed step (Van Schaftingen 1981). Of the four human enzymes that make and break it, PFKFB3 is the one induced by stress and proliferation, and it is almost purely a kinase, turning over fructose 6-phosphate some seven hundred times faster than it hydrolyses the bisphosphate, so where PFKFB3 is present the concentration of fructose 2,6-bisphosphate rises (Sakakibara 1997). Signalling reaches the enzyme on two timescales. Hypoxia-inducible factor 1 binds hypoxia response elements in the PFKFB3 promoter and raises the amount of enzyme in low oxygen and in cells that cannot degrade HIF-1alpha (Minchenko 2002, Obach 2004). The enzyme already present is switched on by phosphorylation of Ser461, which AMP-activated protein kinase carries out when oxidative phosphorylation falters in activated human monocytes (Marsin 2002) and which the p38-activated kinase MK2 carries out within minutes of osmotic, oxidative or genotoxic stress in tumour cells (Novellasdemunt 2013). Both inputs act on the same residue, so transcriptional induction and phosphorylation add up to a sustained rise in glycolytic flux. The converse holds where the enzyme is kept absent. In neurons the ubiquitin ligase APC/C-Cdh1 degrades PFKFB3 continuously, holding glycolysis low and directing glucose into the pentose phosphate pathway (Herrero-Mendez 2009).
