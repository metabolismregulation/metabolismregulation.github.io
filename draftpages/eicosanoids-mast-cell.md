## Description

In mast cells, antigen that cross-links IgE bound to the high-affinity IgE receptor FcεRI starts eicosanoid production, mainly of prostaglandin D2 and leukotrienes. The receptor-associated kinase Lyn phosphorylates the receptor, and Syk, bound to the phosphorylated receptor, is activated and phosphorylates the adaptor LAT. Phospho-LAT recruits phospholipase Cγ1, which is activated by phosphorylation and splits PI(4,5)P2 into IP3 and diacylglycerol. IP3, together with cytosolic Ca2+, opens the IP3 receptor and releases Ca2+ from the endoplasmic reticulum. The emptied store activates STIM1, which opens ORAI1 channels in the plasma membrane, and this store-operated Ca2+ entry sustains the Ca2+ rise. Diacylglycerol activates protein kinase C, which activates RAF1 and the ERK cascade. Ca2+ binds the C2 domain of cytosolic phospholipase A2 (cPLA2α, PLA2G4A), two ions per domain, and phosphorylation by ERK1/2 or p38α at Ser505 and by MNK1 at Ser727 activates the enzyme, which mast cells need for both the immediate and the delayed phase of eicosanoid generation. cPLA2α releases arachidonate from phosphatidylcholine, cyclooxygenases turn it into prostaglandin H2, and hematopoietic prostaglandin D synthase converts this to prostaglandin D2. Pyrrophenone is a selective cPLA2α inhibitor.
