## Description

In peripheral sensory neurons PGE2 released during joint inflammation binds the EP4 receptor, a Gs-coupled GPCR that activates adenylate cyclase and raises cAMP, driving PKA-dependent sensitisation of nociceptor channels such as TRPV1 and NaV1.8. In osteoarthritis models and in human and canine OA, EP4 on peripheral nerve is a principal route by which subchondral and synovial PGE2 produces pain, and EP4 antagonism reduces OA pain. EP4 also drives cartilage matrix catabolism, so it links structural disease and pain.

## Colour

Each block is coloured by its effect on pain, red where it raises pain and blue where it relieves it, and everything in it takes that colour, GTP and GDP included, as it works for the block's result. Drugs are yellow and classes of molecules white.

This block is red: EP4 raises cAMP and sensitises nociceptors.
