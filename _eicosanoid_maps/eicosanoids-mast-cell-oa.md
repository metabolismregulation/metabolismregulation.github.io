## Description

Mast cells release arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids through cytosolic phospholipase A2 and make mainly prostaglandin D2 and leukotriene C4. Cyclooxygenases 1 and 2 turn arachidonic acid into PGG2 and then PGH2, from which specific synthases and reductases make PGE2, PGD2, PGF2alpha, PGI2 and thromboxane A2. The lipoxygenases ALOX5, ALOX12 and ALOX15 make the hydroperoxy and hydroxy acids, the leukotrienes LTB4 and LTC4 to LTE4, lipoxins, eoxins and hepoxilins. ALOX5 needs its activating protein ALOX5AP (FLAP) to make leukotrienes. Cytochrome P450 epoxygenases such as CYP2J2 turn arachidonic acid into the epoxyeicosatrienoic acids (EETs), and soluble epoxide hydrolase (EPHX2) converts them into their diols and also hydrolyses the hepoxilins into trioxilins. HPGD oxidises PGE2 to 15-keto-PGE2, which PTGR2 reduces further, and PGD2 dehydrates into the J2 prostaglandins, among them 15d-PGJ2. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and paracetamol inhibit the cyclooxygenase reaction, and corticosteroids reduce the release of arachidonic acid. Many of the products leave the cell, PGE2 through the exporter ABCC4.
