## Description

Mast cells release arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids through cytosolic phospholipase A2 and make mainly prostaglandin D2 and leukotriene C4 (Boyce 2007). Cyclooxygenases 1 and 2 turn arachidonic acid into PGG2 and then PGH2, from which specific synthases and reductases make PGE2, PGD2, PGF2alpha, PGI2 and thromboxane A2. The lipoxygenases ALOX5, ALOX12 and ALOX15 make the hydroperoxy and hydroxy acids, the leukotrienes LTB4 and LTC4 to LTE4, lipoxins, eoxins and hepoxilins. ALOX5 needs the 5-lipoxygenase-activating protein FLAP to make leukotrienes (Dixon 1990), and mice without FLAP resist collagen-induced arthritis (Griffiths 1997). Cytochrome P450 epoxygenases such as CYP2J2 turn arachidonic acid into the epoxyeicosatrienoic acids (EETs) (Wu 1996). Soluble epoxide hydrolase (EPHX2) converts them into their diols and also hydrolyses the hepoxilins into trioxilins (Cronin 2011). HPGD oxidises PGE2 to 15-keto-PGE2, which PTGR2 reduces further (Chou 2007), and PGD2 dehydrates into the J2 prostaglandins, among them 15d-PGJ2 (Shibata 2002). Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and paracetamol inhibit the cyclooxygenase reaction, and corticosteroids reduce the release of arachidonic acid. PGE2, PGI2, LTB4, PGD2, PGA2, 15d-PGJ2, hepoxilin A3, 12(S)- and 15(S)-HpETE, 5(S)- and 15(S)-HETE and 5,6-EET leave the cell, PGE2 through the exporter ABCC4 (Reid 2003), and act on receptors of the nociceptor.

## Colour

Red raises pain, blue relieves it, and olive is shared signalling whose effect is set by the receptor upstream. Drugs are yellow and classes of molecules white. Enzymes take the colour of what they make or remove, step by step. Each molecule is coloured by its role in this block.

Pain decides the colour, and inflammation where a mediator has no pain data. Red are the prostaglandins E2, I2 and F2alpha, the HpETEs and HETEs, the hepoxilins and the leukotrienes, which sensitise nociceptors or drive inflammation, and the enzymes that make them. PGA2 causes pain through TRPA1 (Materazzi 2008), 5(S)-HpETE and 5-oxo-15(S)-HETE activate neutrophils and eosinophils (Hall 1998, Powell 1995), and 12(R)-HETE recruits neutrophils (Fretland 1989). Blue are the lipoxins and 15-epi-lipoxins, which stop neutrophil infiltration and attenuate inflammatory pain (Svensson 2007), and the enzymes that inactivate red mediators. Blue are also 5-oxo-12(S)-HETE, which blocks 5-oxo-ETE on neutrophils (Powell 1999), 12-HHT, which speeds epithelial repair through BLT2 (Iwamoto 2017), and GPX4, which lowers leukotriene synthesis in a mast cell line (Imai 1998). FLAP is red with the leukotrienes it serves. The EETs are blue, as they relieve inflammatory pain and inhibitors of EPHX2 relieve osteoarthritis pain in dogs (Inceoglu 2008, McReynolds 2019), except 5,6-EET, which causes pain through TRPA1 (Sisignano 2012). EPHX2 is red where it removes the EETs and blue where it inactivates the hepoxilins. Olive are the intermediates, the inactive products, the molecules without data on pain or inflammation and those acting both ways. PGD2 relieves allodynia through DP1 and drives it through DP2 (Minami 1997, Kanda 2013), L-PGDS is needed for allodynia and protects cartilage in osteoarthritis (Eguchi 1999, Najar 2020), and the PGJ2 product 15d-PGJ2 relieves joint pain and also activates TRPA1 (Pena-dos-Santos 2009, Materazzi 2008).

ALOX5 and ALOX15 carry both colours, each step coloured by what it makes. ALOX5 starts the leukotrienes (red), and from 15(S)-HETE made by ALOX15, or 15(R)-HpETE made by cyclooxygenase 2 acetylated by aspirin, it makes the lipoxins and 15-epi-lipoxins (blue) (Clària 1995). ALOX15 leads to 12(S)- and 15(S)-HpETE and the eoxins (red) and to the lipoxins (blue). Which side prevails changes during inflammation: in its resolution PGE2 switches neutrophils from leukotriene to lipoxin synthesis (Levy 2001). The two colours on one enzyme mark a fork in the pathway: inhibiting the enzyme removes mediators on both sides, and its net effect on pain depends on the balance between them.
