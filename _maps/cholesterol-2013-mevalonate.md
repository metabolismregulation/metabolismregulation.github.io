## Selection

The mevalonate arm of the cholesterol biosynthesis map published in 2013 (Mazein 2013, Fig. 1), shown twice: first as published, drawn from the paper's CellDesigner file, then corrected after a review of the literature and of the pathway databases. In the corrected map everything changed is red, and steps that are likely but not shown directly are uncertain processes with dashed lines.

## Description

In human cells mevalonate kinase, phosphomevalonate kinase and mevalonate diphosphate decarboxylase are cytosolic enzymes (Hogenboom 2004a, 2004b, 2004c), and fibroblasts without peroxisomes synthesise cholesterol at normal rates (Hogenboom 2003). The route from mevalonate to farnesyl and geranylgeranyl diphosphate therefore runs in the cytosol, and HMG-CoA reductase and squalene synthase act from the endoplasmic reticulum membrane on cytosolic substrates. A peroxisomal route proposed in rodent and hamster cells (Kovacs 2007) is not drawn beyond its first steps, which remain uncertain: a peroxisomal acetoacetyl-CoA thiolase, an HMG-CoA synthase encoded by the cytosolic synthase gene (Charles 2020) and an HMG-CoA lyase found in human liver peroxisomes (Ashmarina 1994). Acetyl units leave peroxisomes as acetylcarnitine (Wanders 2016). Mitochondrial acetyl-CoA reaches the cytosol as citrate, carried by the citrate carrier and cleaved by ATP citrate lyase, whose inhibition lowers sterol synthesis in human hepatocytes (Ferramosca 2014, Pinkosky 2016). Cytosolic acetyl-CoA also comes from acetate through acetyl-CoA synthetase 2 (Luong 2000). Ketone bodies made in mitochondria feed cholesterol synthesis as well: acetoacetate is activated in the cytosol by acetoacetyl-CoA synthetase (Endemann 1982, Hasegawa 2012, Bergstrom 2023), while its carrier out of mitochondria is unknown (Puchalska 2017). Geranylgeranyl diphosphate synthase acts almost only on farnesyl diphosphate (Sagami 1994).
