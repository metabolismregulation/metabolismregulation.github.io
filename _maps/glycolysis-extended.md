## Description

Fructose 2,6-bisphosphate is the strongest activator of phosphofructokinase-1 and an inhibitor of fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, so it turns the cycle between fructose 6-phosphate and fructose 1,6-bisphosphate towards glycolysis. In the heart it is made and broken down by the bifunctional enzyme PFKFB2. Phosphorylation of PFKFB2 at Ser466 and Ser483 raises its kinase activity and with it the level of fructose 2,6-bisphosphate. Three signals converge on these sites. Adrenaline acts through β-adrenergic receptors, Gs and adenylyl cyclase, and cAMP releases the catalytic subunit of protein kinase A, which phosphorylates both sites. Insulin acts through its receptor, IRS1 and phosphoinositide 3-kinase, and AKT1, activated by PDPK1 at the membrane, phosphorylates both sites as well, while PTEN removes the lipid signal. Energy stress raises AMP, which promotes the activation of AMP-activated protein kinase by LKB1. AMPK phosphorylates Ser466 only, and this stimulates glycolysis in the ischaemic heart.
