## Description

Arachidonate 5-lipoxygenase (ALOX5) converts arachidonate in two steps, through 5-hydroperoxyeicosatetraenoic acid, into leukotriene A4, the precursor of all leukotrienes; the map draws the two steps as one process. Ca2+ binding to the N-terminal domain stimulates the enzyme, and zileuton, a drug approved for asthma, inhibits it.

Phosphorylation switches ALOX5 in both directions. MAPKAP kinase 2 (MAPKAPK2), activated by p38α in stress and inflammatory signalling, phosphorylates ALOX5 at Ser272 and makes it active without a Ca2+ signal; arachidonate itself promotes this phosphorylation. Protein kinase A, released from its regulatory subunits by cAMP (raised by prostaglandin E2, β-adrenergic agonists or adenosine), phosphorylates Ser524 and inhibits catalysis. Because the two marks act in opposite directions, the state variables on ALOX5 carry their sites, in UniProt numbering; the literature numbers the same residues Ser271 and Ser523.
