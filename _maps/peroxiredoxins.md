## Description

The typical 2-Cys peroxiredoxins PRDX1 and PRDX2 are abundant cytosolic peroxidases that reduce hydrogen peroxide with electrons from thioredoxin, which thioredoxin reductase regenerates with NADPH (Chae 1994, Kang 1998). Their high reactivity makes them a main target of hydrogen peroxide in cells (Peskin 2007). During turnover the catalytic cysteine is occasionally oxidised further to sulfinic acid, which inactivates the enzyme (Yang 2002). This sensitivity is thought to let peroxide rise during signalling, as a floodgate (Wood 2003). Sulfiredoxin restores the active enzyme in a reaction that uses ATP (Biteau 2003, Woo 2003, Chang 2004, Woo 2005), and NRF2 induces both PRDX1 and sulfiredoxin (Kim 2007, Singh 2009). Phosphorylation also turns the enzymes down. After growth factor stimulation Src-family kinases phosphorylate membrane-bound PRDX1 on Tyr194, so peroxide accumulates locally where signalling proteins are concentrated (Woo 2010). CDK1 phosphorylates PRDX1 on Thr90 in mitosis (Chang 2002). In neurons exposed to mitochondrial toxins, CDK5 phosphorylates PRDX2 on Thr89, which lowers its activity and promotes neuronal death (Qu 2007).
