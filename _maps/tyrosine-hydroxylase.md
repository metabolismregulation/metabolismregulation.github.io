## Description

Tyrosine hydroxylase catalyses the rate-limiting step of catecholamine synthesis, converting L-tyrosine to L-DOPA with molecular oxygen and the cofactor tetrahydrobiopterin at a non-heme iron site (Daubner 2011). Aromatic L-amino-acid decarboxylase then makes dopamine, the precursor of noradrenaline and adrenaline. The enzyme is held in check by its own products. Dopamine and the other catecholamines bind tightly to the ferric active site and suppress activity, a brake that keeps synthesis matched to demand (Ramsey 1998, Gordon 2008, Nakashima 1999). Phosphorylation of the regulatory N-terminus releases it. Protein kinase A phosphorylates Ser71, known in the older literature as Ser40, which lowers the affinity for the bound catecholamine about three hundred-fold and restores activity (Daubner 1992, Ramsey 1998, McCulloch 2001). Two further sites prime the enzyme rather than acting on their own. Calcium and calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II phosphorylates Ser19, which recruits 14-3-3 proteins and speeds phosphorylation at Ser71 (Itagaki 1999), and the extracellular signal-regulated kinases phosphorylate Ser62, known as Ser31, which adds a small activity gain and stabilises the protein (Haycock 1992, Lehmann 2006). Protein phosphatase 2A removes the Ser71 phosphate and returns the enzyme to its inhibited state (Ahn 2011, Dunkley 2019).
