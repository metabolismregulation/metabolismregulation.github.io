Selected by literature curation, as part of the extension of the Atlas. Glutamate dehydrogenase is controlled by allosteric effectors and by ADP-ribosylation, regulation that the Reactome-Recon lists do not hold for this enzyme.

## Description

Glutamate dehydrogenase (GLUD1) links amino acid and energy metabolism in the mitochondrial matrix. It reversibly deaminates glutamate to 2-oxoglutarate and ammonium, using NAD+ or NADP+. In mammals the enzyme is a homohexamer under tight allosteric control. GTP and ATP inhibit it, and ADP and leucine activate it (Sener 1980, Smith 2001, Fang 2002). Leucine stimulates insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells through this activation. Dominant mutations that blunt the inhibition by GTP cause the hyperinsulinism-hyperammonaemia syndrome, in which protein meals trigger hypoglycaemia (Stanley 1998, Li 2006). The enzyme is also inhibited by ADP-ribosylation of a cysteine, a reversible modification found in liver and hepatoma mitochondria (Herrero-Yraola 2001, Choi 2005). In beta cells the mitochondrial sirtuin SIRT4 uses NAD+ to ADP-ribosylate glutamate dehydrogenase, which restrains amino acid-stimulated insulin secretion, and calorie restriction relieves this brake (Haigis 2006). Later work found SIRT4 to act mainly as a lysine deacylase, so its role as the ADP-ribosyltransferase of glutamate dehydrogenase remains under discussion (Anderson 2017).
