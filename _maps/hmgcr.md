## Description

HMG-CoA reductase catalyses the rate-limiting step of cholesterol synthesis in the endoplasmic reticulum and is held in check by two mechanisms that work on different timescales. When cellular energy falls, AMP-activated protein kinase phosphorylates the enzyme at Ser872 and inactivates it (Clarke 1990, Sato 1993, Loh 2019). When sterols accumulate, the membrane domain of the reductase binds the ER proteins INSIG1 and INSIG2 (Sever 2003). The cholesterol synthesis intermediate 24,25-dihydrolanosterol and the oxysterol 25-hydroxycholesterol trigger this binding, whereas cholesterol itself does not, and whether lanosterol acts is disputed (Song 2005, Lange 2008, Chen 2019). Recent work shows that such sterols bind the reductase directly (Faulkner 2024). INSIG brings the membrane ubiquitin ligases gp78 and RNF145 to the enzyme, which ubiquitinate Lys248 and Lys89 (Sever 2003, Doolman 2004, Song 2005, Menzies 2018). The ATPase p97 then extracts the ubiquitinated reductase from the membrane for degradation by the proteasome (Morris 2014). Geranylgeraniol, a product of the same pathway, speeds this degradation by releasing the prenyltransferase UBIAD1 from the enzyme (Schumacher 2015). Statins inhibit the reductase by competing with HMG-CoA at its active site (Istvan 2001).
