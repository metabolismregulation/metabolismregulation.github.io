## Description

In adipocytes, stored triacylglycerol is broken down in three steps that release fatty acids and glycerol. Adipose triglyceride lipase removes the first fatty acid at the surface of the lipid droplet (Zimmermann 2004, Haemmerle 2006), hormone-sensitive lipase then hydrolyses diacylglycerol (Haemmerle 2002), and monoglyceride lipase completes the breakdown to glycerol (Karlsson 1997, Taschler 2011). The first lipase needs its coactivator ABHD5, which raises its activity many times over (Lass 2006). In the resting cell perilipin 1 holds ABHD5 on the droplet and away from the lipase (Subramanian 2004, Granneman 2007). When catecholamines raise cyclic AMP, protein kinase A phosphorylates perilipin 1 near its carboxy terminus, which releases ABHD5 to bind and activate the lipase (Miyoshi 2007, Granneman 2009). The small protein G0S2 works against this by binding the lipase and holding it inactive, in human as well as mouse adipocytes (Yang 2010, Schweiger 2012). Because the first lipase prefers the middle ester of the triacylglycerol, the diacylglycerol it leaves is mostly the sn-1,3 species (Eichmann 2012). Mutations in the lipase and in its coactivator cause neutral lipid storage diseases in humans (Lefevre 2001, Fischer 2007).
