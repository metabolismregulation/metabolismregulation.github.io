## Selection

The sterol arms of the cholesterol biosynthesis map published in 2013 (Mazein 2013, Fig. 2): the Bloch and Kandutsch-Russell pathways and the shunt to 24(S),25-epoxycholesterol, shown first as published, drawn from the paper's CellDesigner file, then corrected after a review of the literature and of the pathway databases. In the corrected map everything changed is red, and steps that are likely but not shown directly are uncertain processes with dashed lines.

## Description

The enzymes from squalene to cholesterol work in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane. The lamin B receptor, the second sterol C14 reductase next to TM7SF2, is needed for cholesterol synthesis in human cells (Tsai 2016). Sterol Δ24-reductase acts on zymosterol and on 5α-cholesta-7,24-dien-3β-ol, its best substrate, so that most tissues switch from the Bloch to the Kandutsch-Russell route after the C4 demethylations, while the canonical Kandutsch-Russell route from 24,25-dihydrolanosterol carries little flux (Bae 1997, Mitsche 2015). The epoxide shunt turns 24(S),25-epoxylanosterol into 24(S),25-epoxycholesterol (Nelson 1981, Hubler 2021), its single steps inferred from the Bloch pathway, and CYP46A1 also epoxidises desmosterol (Goyal 2014). STARD1 brings cholesterol to the inner mitochondrial membrane, where CYP27A1 hydroxylates it (Conde de la Rosa 2021). 25-Hydroxycholesterol comes from CH25H and CYP3A4, and from CYP27A1 only as a minor product (Honda 2011). Several names in the 2013 file differed from the published figure or from the chemistry and are corrected.
