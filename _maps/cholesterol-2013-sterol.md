## Selection

The sterol arms of the cholesterol biosynthesis map published in 2013 (Mazein 2013, Fig. 2): the Bloch and Kandutsch-Russell pathways and the shunt to 24(S),25-epoxycholesterol, shown first as published, drawn from the paper's CellDesigner file, then corrected. In the corrected map everything changed is red.

## Description

The compartments of the sterol arms hold: the enzymes from squalene to cholesterol are endoplasmic reticulum membrane proteins, and CYP27A1 is mitochondrial. The correction adds the lamin B receptor, the second sterol C14 reductase next to TM7SF2, which human cells need for cholesterol synthesis (Tsai 2016); it resides in the inner nuclear membrane, which is continuous with the endoplasmic reticulum.

Several names in the CellDesigner file differ from the published figure or from the chemistry and are corrected: 14-demethyllanosterol, 24,25-dihydrolanosterol, 25-hydroxycholesterol, 7-dehydrocholesterol and three 5α-cholest-8-en-3β-ol intermediates of the Kandutsch-Russell arm. In that arm the file named the CYP51A1 product 4,4-dimethyl-5α-cholest-8-en-3β-ol and the TM7SF2 product a 2β-ol; they are now 4,4-dimethyl-5α-cholesta-8,14-dien-3β-ol and 4,4-dimethyl-5α-cholest-8-en-3β-ol.
