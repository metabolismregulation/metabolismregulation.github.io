## Description

Serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT) catalyses the first and rate-limiting step of de novo sphingolipid synthesis in the endoplasmic reticulum, the condensation of L-serine with palmitoyl-CoA to 3-oxosphinganine. 3-Oxosphinganine is reduced to sphinganine by KDSR, N-acylated to dihydroceramide by the ceramide synthases and desaturated to ceramide by DEGS1. The human enzyme is a holocomplex of SPTLC1, SPTLC2, the small subunit SPTSSA and an ORMDL protein (Han 2009, Li 2021, Wang 2021). The three ORMDL proteins are bound in functional excess over SPT and mediate the feedback inhibition of ceramide synthesis by ceramide itself (Siow 2012, Siow 2015). Ceramide binds directly to a site formed by ORMDL3 and SPTLC2 and holds the N terminus of ORMDL3 in the substrate tunnel, so that SPT activity falls as ceramide accumulates (Davis 2019, Wang 2021, Xie 2023). ORMDL3 responds most strongly to ceramide, and ORMDL1 and ORMDL2 more weakly (Xie 2023, Mahawar 2025). Unlike the yeast Orm proteins, which are released by phosphorylation, the mammalian ORMDL proteins are regulated through this direct lipid binding (Breslow 2010, Davis 2019). When ceramide is depleted, ORMDL proteins are degraded by the proteasome (Gupta 2015, Mrkacek 2026). SPTLC1 variants that escape ORMDL regulation cause childhood amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (Mohassel 2021), and genetic variants that raise ORMDL3 expression are linked to childhood asthma (Moffatt 2007).
